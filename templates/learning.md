# <The lesson as a claim>

**TL;DR.** One paragraph a reader can stop after. State the claim, the evidence in one
line, and the boundary in one line.

## Situation

The context that made this matter. Org size and stage, the pain we felt, what we had tried
before. Enough for a reader to judge whether their situation is similar.

## What we tried

The concrete thing we did, described so someone else could reproduce it. Tooling by kind,
rules, automation, cadence. No company, product, or people identifiers.

## What happened

Evidence. Numbers, survey results, incidents, before-and-after, anecdotes. Be honest about
what did not work or what we cannot attribute.

## The lesson

The claim, expanded. Why it holds. What mechanism makes it work. This is the core of the
entry and should be the longest section.

## How we apply it today

The current practice in a few lines. If `status` is `dropped`, say what replaced it.

When this does not apply. What org size, stage, or constraint would change the answer.
What would make us revisit it.
