# Engineering Manager: promotion policy

Source document, anonymized. Written 2025-12, aligned with the
[engineering manifesto](../../principles/engineering-manifesto.md). See
[Promotions](../promotions.md) for how nominations run.

## Purpose

Promotions to Engineering Manager (EM) recognize when an engineer is **already operating as
a manager**, consistently and sustainably.

An EM's effectiveness is measured not by their individual output, but by the **performance,
health, growth, and clarity of the team they lead**.

A promotion confirms what is already visible: the engineer enables the team's success,
guides execution, strengthens systems, and positively influences the broader organization.

## Promotion criteria

### 1. Leadership and managerial competencies

A promotable EM consistently demonstrates:

**Team leadership and execution**

- Guides the team through planning and execution without becoming a bottleneck
- Creates clarity in uncertainty; delegates once direction is set
- Ensures predictable delivery and alignment across stakeholders

**People management and development**

- Keeps engineers engaged, motivated, and supported
- Provides continuous feedback and mentorship
- Helps engineers progress in their careers
- Maintains psychological safety and trust

**Technical leadership**

- Offers credible technical guidance
- Makes thoughtful trade-offs and manages tech debt effectively
- Stays hands-on enough to remain connected without blocking the team

**Communication and alignment**

- Operates with a no-surprises mindset
- Communicates proactively and clearly both upward and downward
- Ensures consistent alignment across engineering and cross-functional teams

**Risk awareness and prevention**

- Identifies risks early and raises them appropriately
- Prevents avoidable issues through proactive improvements
- Ensures no single points of failure in people or systems

A candidate should demonstrate **most of these competencies at a next-level standard**.

### 2. Leadership impact (team and organizational)

Engineering Managers create impact through their teams and through their influence across
the organization.

**Team impact**

- The team reliably delivers outcomes with clarity and quality
- Execution improves over time (speed, stability, communication)
- Team members grow meaningfully under their management
- The team operates effectively even without the EM present

**Organizational and cross-functional impact**

- Leads or meaningfully contributes to cross-team initiatives
- Builds strong partnerships with Product, Data, Customer Success, and Operations
- Creates alignment across teams and functions
- Helps establish engineering-wide practices, processes, or standards

**Systemic and technical impact**

- Improves team workflows, communication patterns, or engineering practices
- Reduces operational load or tech debt through leadership
- Guides technical decisions toward sustainability and maintainability
- Strengthens system resilience and reduces organizational risk

A promotable EM demonstrates **sustained leadership impact across all three areas**.

### 3. Requirements and readiness signals

Before promotion, the candidate should have:

**Management experience**

- Acted as a direct manager for **at least 6 months**
- Demonstrated the ability to support and develop reports

**Leadership track record**

- Led multiple projects through planning, execution, and delivery
- Managed stakeholders effectively across functions
- Supported the team through challenges and uncertainty

**Communication reliability**

- Communicates early, clearly, and consistently
- Maintains alignment and avoids surprise escalations

**Team support**

- Has earned trust from their team
- Upward feedback reflects strong leadership
- No unresolved interpersonal or management issues

These signals confirm that the candidate is already functioning as an Engineering Manager.

### 4. Criticality for the company

Criticality is a supporting factor that includes:

- Ownership of important product or system areas
- Leadership of a team essential to business outcomes
- Influence that improves multiple teams or functions
- Proactive prevention of risk or fragility

Criticality strengthens readiness but **does not replace** the need for strong competencies
and leadership impact.

### 5. Growth opportunities

Even when ready for promotion, EMs should have clear, constructive areas to continue
developing, such as:

- Expanding technical breadth
- Increasing organizational influence
- Improving delegation and empowerment
- Strengthening communication with stakeholders
- Enhancing long-term planning and risk forecasting
- Deepening coaching and mentorship skills

## Summary

An engineer can be promoted to Engineering Manager when:

1. They demonstrate next-level leadership and people management competencies
2. Their team performs effectively through their guidance
3. They have sustained impact across team, organizational, and technical dimensions
4. They meet the required leadership experience and readiness signals
5. They are trusted by their team, peers, and cross-functional partners
6. They provide meaningful leverage to the company as a whole
7. They have clear growth opportunities identified for continued development

A promotion to Engineering Manager acknowledges that the individual is already operating at
that level of leadership.
